<ResearchSection eyebrow="Current problem" title="REMEDIATION IS MORE THAN SEVERITY">

A vulnerable dependency can be known, severe and still difficult to remediate in a real software project. A fix has to exist, the consumer has to be able to adopt it, and the surrounding maintenance history may affect how quickly that remediation becomes practical.

I am studying that decision space rather than treating vulnerability severity as the whole problem.

</ResearchSection>

<ResearchSection eyebrow="Research direction" title="DO UPSTREAM MAINTENANCE SIGNALS ADD USEFUL INFORMATION?">

The direction I am validating is whether historical upstream-maintenance signals add useful predictive information about vulnerable-dependency remediation time **after** accounting for vulnerability, remediation and consumer-project context.

The question is intentionally falsifiable: if upstream history does not add stable value beyond those controls, I do not want to force a more complex model to preserve the idea.

</ResearchSection>

<ResearchSection eyebrow="Current method" title="LITERATURE FIRST, THEN DATA FEASIBILITY">

The work is currently focused on literature validation and an open-data feasibility path. The initial pilot is bounded to npm and treats time-to-remediation as a candidate time-to-event outcome where unresolved observations may be censored.

No model family is selected yet. I want the data, outcome definition and competing literature to be defensible before choosing the modeling technique.

</ResearchSection>
