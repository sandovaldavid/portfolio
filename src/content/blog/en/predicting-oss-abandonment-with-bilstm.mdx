> **Update — September 2026:** this post documents an earlier thesis direction. My current
> research focuses on vulnerable-dependency remediation viability in OSS ecosystems. I’m keeping
> these notes because the change itself is part of what I learned: a research plan should change
> when stronger evidence changes the question.

## What I was exploring

My first thesis direction started from repository activity and asked whether sequential signals
could help identify OSS abandonment risk. BiLSTM was the candidate model because commit, issue and
contributor activity are temporal rather than isolated observations.

That framing helped me learn how quickly a model choice can shape the rest of a study: dataset
construction, labels, observation windows, baselines and evaluation all begin to orbit the model if
I am not careful.

## Why I changed direction

The literature review made the original problem less compelling as a thesis contribution than I
had assumed. Instead of defending the first idea, I changed the research question and moved the
model family back to an open decision.

The current work studies **vulnerable-dependency remediation viability**: whether historical
upstream maintenance signals add useful prospective information about remediation timing beyond
vulnerability, remediation and consumer-project characteristics.

## What carried forward

The earlier direction was still useful. I kept several lessons:

- temporal data needs explicit observation windows and leakage controls;
- labels and outcome definitions deserve as much scrutiny as the model;
- stronger models are not automatically better evidence;
- baselines matter because the useful question is whether a new signal adds value;
- a negative or non-incremental result can be informative if the design is sound.

## The part I want to keep visible

Changing the thesis direction was not lost work. It was a correction produced by reading more,
comparing the problem against existing research and being willing to revise my own hypothesis.
That is also how I want to work as an engineer: make assumptions explicit, test them, and change the
plan when the evidence no longer supports them.

The [research page](/research) now reflects the current thesis direction and the decisions that are
still intentionally open.
