> **Research update — September 2026:** This post documents an earlier thesis direction. My current research has moved to vulnerable-dependency remediation in open-source software, and the research question and method are still being refined. See the [current research page](/research).

## The problem

Open-source repositories can lose active maintenance gradually. A project that looked healthy a year ago can end up with fewer contributions, slower issue resolution and growing maintenance risk for downstream users. At the time, this research direction asked a narrower question: could abandonment risk be predicted from a repository's activity time series?

## Why BiLSTM

Commit frequency, issue resolution rate, and contributor churn are sequential signals. The earlier direction explored whether a **Bidirectional LSTM** could use that sequence structure more effectively than models based on aggregated snapshots.

That was a modeling hypothesis for the previous direction, not a result I established.

## Data & features

The proposed data path used GitHub activity sources to construct per-repository time series such as:

- Commit activity and unique contributors
- Issue open/close behavior
- Pull-request activity
- Contributor concentration proxies

The plan was to label repositories using activity-based abandonment heuristics and then test whether sequential structure added useful signal.

## Baseline comparison

The proposed comparison used simpler machine-learning baselines alongside the BiLSTM and evaluation metrics appropriate for an imbalanced classification problem. The aim was to test whether sequence-aware modeling added value, not to assume that deep learning would win.

This direction was later superseded as the thesis problem was refined. The [current research page](/research) reflects the active work and its present evidence boundaries.
